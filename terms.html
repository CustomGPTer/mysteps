Using MySteps

MySteps is free for personal use. You may install it on your own devices and share the download link. Please don't sell it, or pass off a changed copy as MySteps.

Health and safety

MySteps is for general wellbeing and keeping a record. It isn't a medical device, it doesn't diagnose or treat anything, and it isn't medical advice.

  • Blood pressure ranges follow published NHS, European Society of Cardiology (2024) and American Heart Association (2025) guidance, simplified for home readings. A reading of 180/120 or higher shows a safety message. If a reading worries you, or you feel unwell, please contact your GP, call NHS 111, or call 999 in an emergency.
  • Heart rate from the camera is an estimate and can be affected by movement, light and cold fingers. It isn't suitable for checking a heart condition.
  • Calorie targets are estimates from a standard equation and your activity. They're never set below a safe minimum, but they're not a substitute for advice from a doctor or dietitian, especially if you're pregnant, unwell, under 18, or have, or have had, an eating disorder.
  • Weight goals are never set below the healthy BMI range, and the weekly pace is kept gentle. BMI is a rough guide and doesn't suit everyone.
  • Before you start walking much more than usual, particularly if you have a health condition, please check with your GP. Take care near traffic and on uneven ground, and don't look at your phone while crossing roads.

Food information

Everyday foods come from McCance and Widdowson's composition of foods (Crown copyright). Product information comes from Open Food Facts, a database built by volunteers. Recipes change and mistakes happen, so please check the pack, particularly for allergens: MySteps doesn't show allergen information and shouldn't be relied on for it.

Accuracy

Step counts depend on your phone's sensor and where you carry it. Distance and calories are estimates from your step length and weight. Journey distances are approximate.

Journeys and badges

Journeys are virtual: they follow the distance of a real route in your everyday steps, and don't suggest you walk the real route. Place names are used only to describe the routes.
